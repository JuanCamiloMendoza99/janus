# Prompt evaluation

**Which playbook should Janus ship?** Three variants of the support playbook, each
testing a stated hypothesis, run through `/v1/triage` on a fixed model
(`claude-sonnet-5`, no thinking) over the 17-ticket held-out slice — the slice
that was never read while the prompts were written. The variants and what each one
tests live in [`app/domain/prompts/registry.py`](../../app/domain/prompts/registry.py);
the table below is generated from the committed run.

## The finding

**On the held-out slice the variants are within noise of each other on accuracy.**
Every headline gap in the table is one ticket out of seventeen (5.9 points), which
is not a result you can act on. Both `v2-examples` and `v3-terse` edge the baseline
on classification (64.7% vs 58.8% — one ticket) and on next-action routing (88.2%
vs 76.5% — two), and both give a little back elsewhere: `v2` drops a category
ticket, `v3` drops a severity ticket. Read none of these as a win on its own.

**So the decision falls to the axes that are *not* noise.** Two things are measured
precisely rather than sampled:

- **Prefix tokens.** `v3-terse` is 4,738 tokens against the baseline's 6,531 and
  `v2-examples`' 7,913 — a 27–40% spread, counted with the vendor's tokenizer, not
  estimated. On cost per thousand cached tickets that is $5.69 / $5.80 / $6.33.
- **Failure rate on the larger sample.** A triage that breaks the schema produces
  *no verdict* — the ticket is dropped, not misrouted (ADR-008). On the 38-ticket
  training slice the over-length-`reasoning` failure rate was 1/38 for `v2`, 3/38
  for the baseline and 5/38 for `v3-terse`. The terser the playbook, the less
  surrounding prose models a short answer, and the more often the model overruns
  the 1,000-character `reasoning` limit. On the holdout all three failed once, so
  this is suggestive, not conclusive — but it points one way.

**The judge agrees the free text is a wash.** A separate, stronger model
(`claude-opus-4-8`) graded the `summary` and `reasoning` of every output against a
fixed rubric, blind to the variant and to the gold labels. All three cluster at
4.8–4.9 out of 5; `v2-examples` takes it by a hair on grounded reasoning. Trust
that verdict as far as the calibration below allows: the judge matched hand scores
exactly 78% of the time with a mean error of 0.23 on a 1–5 scale — tight on
faithfulness (90%), looser on reasoning-grounded (50% exact, but near-misses), so
its scores are read as a coarse tie-breaker, not a ranking.

## The recommendation

**Ship `v2-examples`.** With accuracy a statistical tie, the tie-break is which
variant is most *reliable* and best-written, and `v2` wins both: the lowest
failure rate on the larger sample and the best free-text scores, while tying for
the best classification and routing. The trade-off it accepts, stated plainly:
**+21% prefix tokens over the baseline for the lowest dropped-ticket rate** — which
in production is cheaper than it looks, because the playbook is cached. After the
first ticket in a burst the prefix bills at the 0.1× read rate, so the +21% tokens
is roughly +11% cost per thousand tickets ($6.33 vs $5.80), not +21%. The caching
work Phase 3 measured is exactly what makes shipping the richer, more reliable
prompt nearly free.

`v3-terse` is the honourable near-miss and the reason the axis was worth sweeping:
it held accuracy at 40% fewer tokens than `v2` and posted the best confidence
calibration of the three (ECE 0.133). If token cost dominated — a much larger
prefix, or a model with a punishing input rate — it would be the answer. Here the
dropped-ticket risk outweighs an 11% cost saving on an already-cached prefix.

Two caveats that belong next to the numbers:

- **n = 17.** The holdout is small and the accuracy gaps are within its noise. The
  claim is not "`v2` is more accurate"; it is "the variants tie on accuracy, and
  `v2` wins the tie-breakers that are measured rather than sampled."
- **`v2`'s examples were written from the training slice's errors**, so its 92%
  classification *there* is partly overfit and is not evidence of anything. Only
  the holdout numbers above count, and there it merely ties.

**Total spend: $0.30 triage + $0.21 judge = $0.51.**
